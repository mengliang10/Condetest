The premise

A road is a story told in distance.

Kashgar sits at 1,300 metres, at the foot of the Karakoram, within a day’s drive of Pakistan, Afghanistan and Kyrgyzstan. It is often described as the heart of Turkestan identity: a city rooted more in the Islamic cultures of Central Asia than in those of China.

From here the journey becomes a question: how did people, faiths and art cross a desert this vast? Ten days answer it in order. Mountains first, then oasis cities, then the long crossing, then the paintings left in the caves.

Travel not from site to site, but from one layer of history to the next.

Three chapters
Chapter I · Days 1–4

Where the mountains meet cultures.

A convoy of 4×4 Prados climbs the Pamir Plateau past White Sand Lake and the peak known as “the father of the icebergs”, to a picnic beside Karakul Lake. Next morning, sunrise at Golden Grass Beach, then Stone City, once the royal city of the Puli Kingdom, and the Panlong road’s coiling bends.

The heart of the chapter is lunch in a Tajik home, where you can help prepare barbecue and milk tea. Then back to Kashgar’s adobe lanes, where craftspeople give a lifetime to a single craft.

Plan around itThe livestock market runs on Sundays only. Arrive on a Thursday and Day 4 falls on a Sunday.

Chapter II · Days 5–6

The distance between oases.

In Yarkant, once a major Silk Road stop, you visit the Altun Mosque complex and the tomb of Amannisahan, a Uyghur musician and queen. Hotan follows, then 650 kilometres of desert highway beside the Tarim River, through poplar forests and desert villages.

Honest noteDay 6 is about eight hours on the road. It is also the day that gives the caves of Kuqa their meaning.

Ask about this chapter
Chapter III · Days 7–10

The stories left in stone.

The Kizil Caves, 236 grottoes cut into the cliffs, hold murals shaped by influences that travelled the Silk Road. In Urumqi, the regional museum shows people the desert preserved naturally, including the roughly 4,000-year-old “Loulan Beauty”.

The finale is a full day at Tianchi, an alpine lake in the Tianshan, a quiet place to take in what you have crossed.

Honest about the road

Is this journey for you?

A private itinerary should feel right before you book it, so here is what to expect.

It suits you if…

  • History, faith and art are why you travel
  • You enjoy long, scenic drives: about 6, 5.5 and 8 hours on Days 2, 5 and 6
  • You want time with local people, such as the Tajik-home lunch
  • You are comfortable at altitude (Day 2 reaches about 3,600 m)

Think twice if…

  • You prefer minimal time in transit
  • You have heart or breathing conditions: please speak to your doctor first
  • You expect one hotel standard throughout: stays range from a boutique mountain garden hotel to international city hotels

Day by day

Ten days, one unfolding story.

01Arrive in KashgarThe threshold of a different worldKashgar

Your local guide meets you at the airport, and border-control permits are applied for nearby before the hotel transfer. Settle in at 1,300 metres, in one of China’s most remote cities.

Overnight · Radisson Blu Hotel Kashgar

02Kashgar → TashkurganInto the high PamirsAbout 300 km · 6 hr

A convoy of 4×4 Toyota Prados climbs the Pamir Plateau, passing White Sand Lake. Picnic beside Karakul Lake at roughly 3,600 metres, then a yak hot-pot dinner in Tashkurgan.

High altitude: please talk to your doctor before booking if you have heart or breathing conditions.

Overnight · Panoramic Star Hotel

03Tashkurgan → KashgarMountain roads, human storiesPamir region

Sunrise at Golden Grass Beach, then Stone City. Follow the Panlong road’s 200-plus bends and cross the Tasha road past the mouth of the Wakhan Corridor. Lunch is in a Tajik home, where you can help prepare barbecue and milk tea.

Apricot blossom is possible between 23 March and 20 April, but not guaranteed.

Overnight · Radisson Blu Hotel Kashgar

04KashgarThe living cityKashgar

Wander the old town’s adobe lanes and, if time allows, take brick tea with elders on Ustang Boyi street. Visit Id Kah Mosque, China’s largest, and the Abakh Khoja mausoleum. On a Sunday, start at the livestock market.

Overnight · Radisson Blu Hotel Kashgar

05Kashgar → Yarkant → HotanOasis cities of the Silk RoadAbout 500 km · 5.5 hr

Lunch in Yarkant, once an important Silk Road stop, with the Altun Mosque complex, the tomb of Amannisahan and the old town, before continuing to Hotan.

Overnight · Hotan Silk Road Welcome Hotel

06Hotan → KuqaAcross the TaklamakanAbout 650 km · 8 hr

The Taklamakan Desert Highway, with views of the Tarim River, poplar forests and stops in desert villages. The longest day of the journey, and the one that gives what follows its weight.

Overnight · Kuqa Tian Yuan International Hotel

07KuqaThe paintings in the cavesKizil Caves

The Kizil Caves, 236 rock-cut Buddhist grottoes whose murals show influences from across the Silk Road. Then about 80 km (2 hours) to Kuqa Ancient Town, once a great power and a centre of Buddhism; the King’s Mansion if time permits.

Please ask us about cave access and photography rules.

Overnight · Kuqa Tian Yuan International Hotel

08Kuqa → UrumqiArchaeology and the modern cityDomestic flight

A short flight to Urumqi (the source itinerary uses CZ6872, 09:55–11:00; schedule to be confirmed, fare not included). If timing allows, visit the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region Museum and its naturally preserved mummies, including the “Loulan Beauty”.

Overnight · Conrad Urumqi

09Urumqi / Tianchi LakeA quieter horizonTianshan

A full day at Tianchi, the half-moon alpine lake on the slopes of Bogda Peak, with time to hike and to take in the week behind you.

Overnight · Conrad Urumqi

10Departure from UrumqiTake the journey with youDeparture

Breakfast, then transfer to the airport for your flight home.

Good questions

Before you enquire.

Is this a group tour?

No. It is a private, tailor-made journey with an English-speaking local guide, and the route and pace can be tailored to your party.

How demanding is it?

The demands are drives and altitude, not hiking. Days 2, 5 and 6 are about 6, 5.5 and 8 hours on the road, and Day 2 reaches about 3,600 metres at Karakul Lake. If you have heart or breathing conditions, speak to your doctor first.

What about visas and permits?

Border-control permits are applied for near Kashgar airport on arrival. Visa and entry taxes are not included, so tell us your passport when you enquire and we will confirm current requirements.

When is the best time to go?

It depends on what you want to see. Apricot blossom is possible between 23 March and 20 April, and Kashgar’s livestock market runs on Sundays only. Tell us your window and we will advise what to expect.

What is not in the price?

International flights and taxes, the Kuqa–Urumqi domestic flight, visa and entry taxes, travel insurance and personal expenses. A single supplement may apply if you are not sharing a room.

Can I add days or other destinations?

Yes. Extensions and onward travel are arranged separately; tell us what you have in mind.
